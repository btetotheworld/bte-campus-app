Filed by a student on a phone, in a hall, on bad signal, in under three minutes. Same five fields, same Shift confirmation, same optional flags box in all three. Navy, ember, neutrals only; 4px maximum radius.

1A

Single scroll

Optimises for seeing the whole ask at once — a lead can scan it before the room empties and knows exactly how long this will take.

Gives up focus. Five open text fields on one screen invite one-word answers, and the keyboard covers half the form on every tap.

1B

Stepped flow

Optimises for answer quality. One question fills the screen above the keyboard, so the honest sentence gets typed instead of a shrug, and each step commits on its own — a dropped connection at step 5 loses nothing.

Gives up the sense of an end. Seven taps to advance and no view of the whole, so it feels longer than 1A even when it is faster, and a lead cannot skip to the one field they came back to fix.

1C

Roster tap, then one review screen

Optimises for not typing. The first screen is the roster: tap who was there, then long-press-free chips mark who ran the block, who demoed and who is stuck. Attendance and first timers are counted, not entered, and four of the five fields arrive as names. The second screen is the whole report as a review list — the only writing left is the Shift reason and the optional flags box.

Gives up nuance and correctness at the edges. Names are not sentences, so “Chidi” replaces “Chidi, stuck on env vars for three weeks” unless the lead opens the row. It also depends on the roster being current, so a chapter with a stale roster gets a worse form than 1A gives them, and guests who never joined the roster have to be added on the spot.
